\section{Problem and Approach}
Categories are how files on Wikimedia Commons are found, but many uploads arrive with few or none, and
editors must choose from a huge, growing, long-tailed category graph. A fixed classifier fits poorly: the
label space changes daily, most categories have a handful of examples, labels are incomplete (a missing
category is not a negative), and editors need to know \emph{why} a category is suggested and \emph{when}
the system is unsure. CommonsLens treats categories as retrievable objects in a shared image--text space:
a frozen vision--language encoder, several complementary scorers, calibrated fusion, hierarchy-aware
decoding, and conformal suggestion sets, with evidence shown for every suggestion and an editor always in
the loop. Nothing is applied automatically.

\section{What Makes the Difference}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Open vocabulary by design (M1).} Categories are \emph{retrieved}, not classified: a category
is suggestible the moment it is named, and new images or editor decisions count immediately, with no
retraining.
\item \textbf{Many weak signals, one calibrated score (M1).} Five complementary scorers are fused on a few
hundred candidates, never on all categories: +53\% F1@5 over the best single scorer.
\item \textbf{Missing labels are not punished (M1).} Unlabelled parents of true categories are left out of
fusion training instead of counted as negatives, the head uses an asymmetric loss, and decoding is
hierarchy-aware; hierarchy-aware precision is reported next to strict precision.
\item \textbf{Honest about uncertainty (M1, built for the final).} An \emph{Unsure} flag from the content
type, and conformal suggestion sets that send an upload to manual categorisation instead of guessing.
\item \textbf{Explainable (M1).} Every suggestion shows the indexed images that voted for it and the
signals behind its score, in line with Wikimedia's transparency principle.
\item \textbf{Evaluation that matches deployment (M2).} Temporal split, de-duplication and a vocabulary
built only from earlier data cut the validation--test gap from .135 to .014; balanced and uniformly random
samples allow a bias audit.
\item \textbf{Robust to real uploads (M2).} Fusion is trained on copies without titles and with degraded
images: heavy blur costs 5\% F1@5 with a title.
\item \textbf{Built with testers (M2, final).} Hands-on testing and public tester reports drove more
than ten fixes, from centre weighting to a fifth content type, Living thing.
\item \textbf{Open and licence-clean.} Frozen open-weight encoder, MIT code, openly licensed training data
only; it runs on a laptop.
\end{itemize}

\section{System}
Figure~1 shows the pipeline; the encoder is frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 (SigLIP is supported).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Encoder:} L2-normalised embeddings; uploads add a 0.4-weighted embedding of the central 60\%
crop, so the main subject outweighs the background.
\item \textbf{Scorers:} (1) $k$NN voting over indexed images, softmax($s/\tau$), editor rejections as
negative votes; (2) text prototypes from prompt ensembles; (3) \emph{smoothed prototypes} (text embedding
blended with the image centroid, $w{=}n/(n{+}5)$; parent/sibling term tested, Section~4.2); (4) a
text-initialised linear head with asymmetric loss for categories with $\geq$3 images; (5) metadata:
title/description embedding, IDF word overlap, year agreement.
\item \textbf{Fusion:} logistic regression over 17 features on the union of each scorer's top 100,
fitted on validation plus copies with metadata removed (30\%) or images degraded (30\%).
\item \textbf{Hierarchy decoding:} an ancestor is pushed down when a descendant is confident; the strength
is tuned on validation.
\item \textbf{Conformal suggestion sets:} split conformal calibration on validation, separately for uploads
with and without metadata. Members are marked \checkmark; an empty set means ``manual categorisation
recommended''. $\alpha$ is the smallest value whose median set stays $\leq$10.
\item \textbf{Content type first:} Object / Living thing / Scene / Event / Abstract concept, from a
classifier on frozen embeddings (939 openly licensed Commons photos, equal share per source category)
blended with a vote of the suggestions; ``Unsure'' below 50\%, two types shown when close.
\item \textbf{Filters:} maintenance and contest categories are never suggested. The display hides bare
country names, and over-specific categories (dates, people counts, non-Latin script) unless their score
is at least 70\%; evaluation scores the model before these display filters.
\item \textbf{Continual learning:} new categories are suggestible immediately (text prototype, word
match), new images are appended to the index, and editor accept/reject feeds back without retraining.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data and Protocol}
6,303 files from the MediaWiki API: 155 seed categories (107 topical; 48 chosen for balance across
continents) plus 1,500 uniformly random files. Hidden and maintenance categories are
removed. \textbf{Temporal split} by upload time (4,429 / 937 / 937), perceptual-hash and embedding
($\cos\geq0.95$) de-duplication. \textbf{Realistic validation:} the vocabulary is built from training
data (plus seeds and parents) only; validation categories are added after fusion is fitted, giving 24,486
categories at test time; the validation--test F1@5 gap is .014, so validation is a faithful guide.
72\% of test labels belong to categories with no training image, and only 36\% of test labels are in the
vocabulary at all, which bounds recall. Encoder: CLIP ViT-B/32; $K{=}50$, $\tau$ tuned on validation.

\subsection{Content type}
The classifier is trained on 939 photos (Object 175, Living thing 216, Scene 193, Event 176, Abstract
179) and reaches 82.9\% on held-out photos across the five types. On a hand-labelled pack of 250
living subjects (88 of them deliberately hard: partly hidden, old or low-contrast photos), 91.6\% were typed
Living thing and 88.6\% of the hard ones got the right broad type. Portraits were the exception (4 of 14),
traced to a collection bug that left the people source categories unused; it is fixed and the classifier
retrained. Test-pack photos are never used for training.

\subsection{Newest uploads (deployment distribution)}
To see what a deployment would face, we sampled 480 files uploaded 7--30 days before evaluation,
from 25 random moments with at most five per uploader and none from the training data. Only
\textbf{2.0\%} of their labels exist in our 24,486-category vocabulary (36\% on the seed-based test set),
so F1@5 is .002 and R@10 .004, about a fifth of what is reachable. On real uploads accuracy is capped by
\emph{vocabulary coverage}, not by the model: a deployment must index the full Commons category list.
CommonsLens can add categories without retraining (text prototype and title matching, our strongest
single signal), so this is a data step, but a necessary one.

\section{Findings}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fusion is the main win:} .152 vs .099 for the best single scorer (+53\%).
\item \textbf{Metadata is essential for places and robustness:} Africa .207 with a title vs .006 image only;
under heavy blur F1@5 drops 5\% with metadata but 36\% image only.
\item \textbf{The long tail is the bottleneck:} recall@5 .821 for categories with $\geq$20 images, .003 for
unseen ones, which hold 72\% of test labels. Neither text prototypes nor graph smoothing closed this gap,
and on the newest uploads only 2\% of labels are in the vocabulary.
\item \textbf{Regional disparity:} with metadata, Europe .274 and Africa .207 but North America .036;
image-only accuracy is near zero for every region. Groups are small (42--71 images), so the gaps are
indicative only.
\item \textbf{Evaluation pitfalls we caught:} a validation vocabulary that included validation labels
(val--test gap .135$\rightarrow$.014 once fixed), and a display-only filter wrongly applied inside the
model, which silently zeroed the scores for photos from several regions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Testing}
77 automated tests (offline end-to-end train $\rightarrow$ evaluate $\rightarrow$ predict $\rightarrow$
update, conformal coverage on synthetic data, smoothing, collectors, demo). Hands-on testing with our own
photos and tester reports from the public demo led to: centre weighting (people pulled towards
``Marathons''), buildings as Scenes, Living thing as a fifth type, mixed types (a fruit market is
Object + Scene), hiding dated and non-Latin categories, filtering ``Wiki Loves'' contest categories, and
de-duplicating repeated reports. The last round (a car labelled ``Mini automobiles in Germany'', a football
photo labelled with a photographer's collection) added a broad-category row and hides collections,
container categories (``X by Y''), counts and country-level variants unless the title names the country.
A single person was matched to ``Holi'' because most people photos in our index come from that festival;
fixing this needs more varied training data.

\section{Responsible Use and Licensing}
Only openly licensed Commons files are used for training and indexing, and each keeps its licence.
External datasets suggested during development (ObjectNet, WIDER FACE, DTD, COCO) were \emph{not} used
for training: their terms forbid training on them, limit use to non-commercial research, or mix such
licences, which would conflict with the MIT licence and the sponsor's commercial licence. CommonsLens never identifies people; it only types them as
living things. Suggestions are always reviewed by an editor, with the evidence images shown.

\section{Editor Experience}
Each result starts with a {\color{accent}$\star$} row giving the content type (or two, e.g.\ a fruit
market is Object + Scene) and an \emph{Unsure} banner when the type is below 50\%, then a broad category
(e.g.\ Automobiles) from a zero-shot match against about a hundred broad Commons categories. Suggestions follow with
their score; rows marked \checkmark\ form the conformal set, and notes explain abstention, a missing title
or a low-quality image. For every suggestion the demo shows the indexed images that voted for it and the
signals behind its score. Editors can accept or reject suggestions and add new categories; testers can
report a problem, which is stored with what the system showed. A public read-only mode hides the editing
panels.

\section{Deployment and Scalability}
The encoder is frozen, so no GPU training is needed and a new deployment only builds an index. Scoring
and ranking take 1\,ms per image after encoding; the index is a FAISS flat, HNSW or IVF-PQ index chosen by
configuration, and candidates keep fusion independent of the number of categories. On Commons the
system would run asynchronously on upload events and store suggestions with their evidence for editors;
nothing blocks or changes an upload. New categories, new images and editor decisions take effect
immediately; the fusion model and conformal thresholds are refitted periodically on recent uploads.

\section{Reproducibility}
Every number in this report comes from the repository's scripts (\texttt{prepare\_data.py},
\texttt{train.py}, \texttt{evaluate.py}, \texttt{train\_content\_type.py}) with a fixed seed; the README
lists the commands and links to the built artifacts. All code is MIT-licensed; third-party models,
libraries and data are credited in the README.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
Only 36\% of test labels (2\% for the newest uploads) are reachable, so the first step is indexing the
full Commons category list, then the tail (multilingual category names, geotags, mining sibling categories
with editors). Also pending: a SigLIP vs CLIP comparison (supported in the code), a human-reviewed score
for ``wrong'' suggestions that are actually valid, and rolling conformal recalibration.
